Limitations¶

What the driver does not do, what it assumes where the datasheet is silent, and what no test here can confirm. Every assumption has a knob in Config and a model knob in the tests, and every open question has an owner decision or a bench step. The driver is not verified on hardware: everything below is tested against a model written from the datasheet, never against a real sensor.

Datasheet sections this page relies on

From Microchip Technology Inc. DS20001942 L:

  • Accuracy of each device: DC Electrical Characteristics, Sensor Accuracy, p. 3
  • Thermal response: DC Electrical Characteristics (t_RES), p. 4
  • Thermal considerations and self-heating: 4.4 Thermal Considerations, p. 11

All of them, with the driver API for each: Datasheet map.

What it does not do¶

  • No correction for nonlinearity, supply or self-heating. The result is the first-order equation of the datasheet; the error budget is on Transfer function. For better accuracy than ±0.5 °C typical after calibration, compensate with a table (the datasheet points to AN1001).
  • No gain trim. One point calibrates the offset only.
  • No detection of a floating pin that holds a stable level. A pin with no sensor that sits at a steady mid-scale voltage (a leaking ADC input, crosstalk, a held sample-and-hold capacitor) passes every check: 1650 mV reads as 115 °C on an MCP9700. A stuck-at-0 line (OutputLow), a rail-high line (Saturated) and a moving line (Unstable) are caught. On the board, add a 100 kΩ pull-down at the ADC pin so an open line reads OutputLow, or compare the first reading with a known temperature (O-14). The range window is a property of the part, so it cannot be narrowed to the climate of one product without a knob; range_margin_mc shrinks it.
  • A grounded line is caught only below the window floor (20 mV or 49 mV by default, A-11).
  • No detection of the part. The five devices cannot be told apart at run time; the declaration is yours.
  • No detection of an ADC that is not what you declared. A full scale that is too high hides a clip.
  • No power measurement. The operating current (6 µA typical) is a documented value, not a reading.
  • No retries. A reading that fails is reported once; call again if you want another.
  • Not thread-safe. One owner, or one mutex.

Custom parts¶

Custom serves another linear active thermistor IC whose output is first order. It is a declaration, not a verified part: no test can know your sensor. A wrong V0, TC or range gives wrong temperatures that look plausible. The two limits to know:

  • 0 mV at the minimum (O-12). If V0 + TC x min is exactly 0 mV, a reading of 0 mV is a valid reading at the minimum, so a grounded or unpowered line is not caught by the window at any margin. Check at a known temperature at start-up, or add a pull-up on the board.
  • Positive outputs only (O-13). A part whose output goes below 0 V at its minimum, or whose slope is negative, is rejected by Validate().
