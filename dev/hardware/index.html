Hardware¶

The MCP9700, MCP9700A, MCP9700B, MCP9701 and MCP9701A: the packages and pins, the output, the ADC in front of it, the timing, the accuracy and the supply, as the driver uses them. Values are from the datasheet; when your schematic disagrees on pin numbers, the schematic wins.

Datasheet sections this page relies on

From Microchip Technology Inc. DS20001942 L:

  • Pins and packages: Package Types; 3.0 Pin Descriptions, Table 3-1, p. 2
  • Absolute maximum ratings: 1.0 Electrical Characteristics, p. 3
  • Supply range and current: DC Electrical Characteristics, p. 3
  • Output current, impedance and load capacitance: DC Electrical Characteristics (I_OUT, Z_OUT, C_LOAD), p. 4
  • Turn-on time: DC Electrical Characteristics (t_ON), p. 4
  • Specified temperature ranges: Temperature Characteristics, p. 4
  • Output voltage range and an output outside it: 3.2 Output Voltage Pin (VOUT), p. 9
  • Shutdown from an I/O pin: 4.2 Shutdown Using Microcontroller I/O Pin, p. 11
  • Layout and decoupling: 4.3 Layout Considerations, p. 11
  • Thermal considerations and self-heating: 4.4 Thermal Considerations, p. 11
  • Packaging drawings and ordering codes: 5.0 Packaging Information; Product Identification System, p. 13
  • The five devices and the packages each comes in: Product Identification System; 5.1 Package Marking, p. 23

All of them, with the driver API for each: Datasheet map.

The family¶

The datasheet lists exactly five devices. They are one first-order sensor in two scalings and three accuracy grades; the driver holds each as one row of a table (mcp9700::kVariantTable) and never branches on a name.

Device V0 at 0 °C Slope TC Specified range Supply Accuracy tested at Packages
MCP9700 500 mV 10.0 mV/°C -40 to +125 °C (+150 °C High) 2.3 to 5.5 V VDD = 3.3 V SOT-23-3, SC70-5, TO-92-3
MCP9700A 500 mV 10.0 mV/°C -40 to +125 °C 2.3 to 5.5 V VDD = 3.3 V SOT-23-3, SC70-5, TO-92-3
MCP9700B 500 mV 10.0 mV/°C -40 to +125 °C (+150 °C High) 2.3 to 5.5 V VDD = 3.3 V SOT-23-3, SC70-5
MCP9701 400 mV 19.5 mV/°C -10 to +125 °C 3.1 to 5.5 V VDD = 5.0 V SOT-23-3, SC70-5, TO-92-3
MCP9701A 400 mV 19.5 mV/°C -10 to +125 °C 3.1 to 5.5 V VDD = 5.0 V SOT-23-3, SC70-5, TO-92-3

The High option (ordering code H, up to +150 °C) exists for the MCP9700 and MCP9700B in SOT-23-3 and SC70-5 only (DC Electrical Characteristics, note 1, p. 3). The automotive-qualified VAO ordering suffix selects a qualification, not a different device, so it is not a variant. The parts cannot be told apart at run time, so the variant is something you declare in Config; the driver does not check the declaration (open question O-3 in Limitations).

The datasheet has no block diagram. Section 4.0 (p. 10) describes it: an internal diode measures the die temperature, and its voltage is scaled to the output. The blocks above follow that description and are marked unverified in the file for that reason.

Circuits¶

The datasheet needs no part other than the sensor, and recommends 0.1 µF to 1 µF between VDD and GND, close to the pins (section 4.3, p. 11).

Power from an I/O pin¶

Section 4.2 (p. 11) allows the sensor to be powered from an MCU pin, which lets the driver switch it off (Sleep()). The pin's switching noise reaches the sensor as supply noise and spoils the accuracy, so the datasheet asks for a decoupling capacitor and a series resistor: 200 Ω with 1 µF at VDD (section 4.3). Put the resistor in the supply line; never in series with VOUT, which would add an error with the ADC's input current.

The port drives the pin INACTIVE (unpowered) before it enables the pin's output, and EnsureInitialized() powers the sensor with GpioSet(CtrlPin::Enable, GpioSignal::ACTIVE). See Porting.

The output¶

Equation 4-1 (p. 10): VOUT = TC x TA + V0 °C. How the driver turns a voltage into a temperature, with the rounding and the numbers, is on Transfer function.

Thermal response tRES 1.3 s (SC70 on a 1 x 1 inch board), 1.65 s (TO-92 in a fluid bath), to 63 % 4 caps the averaging window

The output is not ratiometric to VDD: a 0.1 °C/V line regulation means the supply barely moves the reading, so the driver needs the voltage at VOUT and nothing about the supply. An ADC that measures against its own supply does not change that, as long as its port reports calibrated millivolts.

The datasheet does not give the output level of an unpowered or shut-down sensor, so the driver never relies on it. EnsureInitialized() waits for the turn-on time and then checks the first reading; a sensor that is still off reads as OutputLow or Unstable. The bench step to learn what your board shows is in Troubleshooting.

The ADC in front of it¶

The driver needs two numbers from you: the full scale of your port (the largest millivolt value it reports) and its resolution in bits (Config::adc_full_scale_mv, Config::adc_resolution_bits). They set the size of one step, and they let the driver tell a clipped conversion from a real one: a conversion within one step of the full scale is Saturated, never a temperature. The sensor's own output never reaches the full scale of a sensible setup, so a clip is always a fault (an open input, a wrong attenuation or a mis-declared scale).

ADC Full scale One step at the pin Step in temperature (9700) (9701)
8 bit 2500 mV 9.8 mV 977 m°C
10 bit 3300 mV 3.2 mV 323 m°C
12 bit 3100 mV 0.76 mV 76 m°C
12 bit 3300 mV 0.81 mV 81 m°C 42 m°C
12 bit 4096 mV 1.0 mV 100 m°C
12 bit 5000 mV 1.22 mV 63 m°C
16 bit 3300 mV 0.05 mV 6 m°C

ResolutionMilliC() returns the step for your configuration, rounded up. Averaging eight conversions lowers the noise but not the step; the sensor's own accuracy (±1 °C to ±4 °C) is larger than any step above 10 bits.

The ADC's input must not draw more than 100 µA on average, including the charge of its sample-and-hold, and VOUT should see no more than 1000 pF. If your ADC is a multiplexed one, its sample capacitor holds the previous channel's voltage after a switch: the first conversion is wrong. The driver discards discard_conversions conversions (default 1) before each reading for that reason. A voltage divider in front of the ADC is declared with divider_num and divider_den; the driver scales back to the voltage at VOUT.

Accuracy¶

Sensor accuracy is the error limit the datasheet guarantees over a span at its tested supply (p. 3). The driver holds the limits (AccuracyLimitsFor()) so you can check a measurement against them, but it never applies them to a reading.

Device Span Error limits
MCP9700B +20 to +70 °C -1 to +1 °C
MCP9700B 0 to +125 °C -2 to +3 °C
MCP9700B -40 to +125 °C -2 to +4 °C
MCP9700B, High option -40 to +150 °C -4 to +4 °C
MCP9700A, MCP9701A 0 to +70 °C -2 to +2 °C
MCP9700A -40 to +125 °C -2 to +4 °C
MCP9701A -10 to +125 °C -2 to +4 °C
MCP9700, MCP9701 0 to +70 °C -4 to +4 °C
MCP9700 -40 to +125 °C -4 to +6 °C
MCP9701 -10 to +125 °C -4 to +6 °C
MCP9700, High option -40 to +150 °C -4 to +6 °C

The narrowest span that contains the temperature wins. The datasheet's High-option rows cover the whole range to +150 °C and it does not say that an H part also meets the tighter Extended rows (p. 3 labels only the -40 to +150 °C rows "High Temperature"). So for TemperatureClass::High the driver uses the High-option rows alone, over the whole range: the guarantee it reports is never tighter than the one the datasheet gives.

Self-heating at 25 °C with the maximum supply, current and load is 0.179 °C (section 4.4, p. 11): it is left in the reading. The sensor measures the die, which follows the board through the pins far more than the air; for the air, use the TO-92 package (section 4.4).
